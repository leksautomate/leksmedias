# Copywriting notes

## 1. Positioning

**We do the work for them.** We are not selling prompts, skill files or a system to learn. The customer gives a topic and gets a finished, researched, checked script.

| | Sells the system | Leksmedia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | Files the buyer installs and runs | A finished script, delivered |
| Buyer's job | Set up, pick a model, prompt, audit | Brief, review, edit |
| AI cost | Buyer's own subscription | Included in credits |
| Research | Buyer gathers sources | We research the live web and cite it |

One line to reuse everywhere: **"Not a prompt pack. A finished script."**

The landing page now says this in the "Done for you" section and the FAQ ("Do I get the prompts or skill files? No.").

## 2. What we learned from the pasted sales page, and what we did not copy

A competitor's page was pasted in. It is a good teardown of how this market sells. We took **structure and ideas only**, wrote everything in our own words, and did **not** reuse any of its claims.

| Idea | How we used it |
| --- | --- |
| Comparison against "just use a chat AI" | Section "Why not just use a chat AI?" with seven rows (research, facts, hook, long scripts, visual cues, batches, cleanup) |
| Comparison against the other kind of product | Section "Not a prompt pack. A finished script." (our angle: done for you vs do it yourself) |
| "For you / Not for you" | Section "Is this for you?" Honest exclusions build trust, including "You want to own and run a prompt system yourself" |
| "You'll know within your first script" | Free first script plus a "Read the sample" button |
| Plain, specific objection answers | Expanded FAQ |
| Numbers near the top | Stats band (below) |

**Do not reuse these, they are another company's claims:** "85% vs 38% retention", "200M+ views", "10,000+ scripts", "100+ niches tested", "400+ creators", and its version history or member names. Publishing them as ours would be false advertising, and repeating a competitor's testimonials or screenshots is also an IP problem.

## 3. Claims policy

**We can say now (true today, from the product spec or verified docs):**
- 6 script formats, longest script 30,000 words, written section by section
- 2 outputs per run (visual cues, clean with sources)
- Deep research runs typically take 5 to 20 minutes (TinyFish docs)
- Every claim is mapped to a source in the fact-check list (this is the design rule; keep the wording as a design statement until the checker is built and tested)
- "Your first script is free" (plan: 300 free credits, about 2 standard scripts)

**We must not say until we have evidence:**
- Any retention figure, view count, "proven to go viral", "scripts that go viral"
- "Sounds like you" until voice profiles exist
- Any number of scripts or creators served
- Comparisons that name a competitor's weaknesses we have not verified

**How to get real retention stats (the thing the pasted page leans on):**
1. In the beta, give 20 to 30 creators free credits in exchange for permission to share results.
2. For each, record the script version, topic, length and publish date.
3. After 7 to 14 days, collect YouTube Studio retention screens: percent still watching at 0:30, average view duration, average percentage viewed. Ask for the same channel's previous 3 videos as a baseline.
4. Publish only real before and after screenshots with the creator's consent, and state the sample size and caveats ("results vary with topic, thumbnail, title and edit").
5. Replace the "Product specs" stats band caption with a results band once there are at least 10 consented examples.

## 6. Tone rules

- Plain and specific. Name the thing and what it costs.
- No hype words ("powerful", "game-changing", "revolutionary"), no "proven to go viral".
- No em dashes, no "Here's the thing:" style setup phrases, no "Most people..." openers. (These are the same AI tells the product removes from scripts, so the page must pass its own audit.)
- Avoid "you need to understand" and "before you understand" phrasing.
- Honest exclusions in "Not for you".

## 7. Legal and licensing reminder

"We are not selling the skill" changes how we position the product. It does **not** settle the license question. The rulebook the writing system is built on was sold as a product; its terms may allow use for your own channel but not running it as a service for other people's scripts, or exposing its rules through MCP. Get written permission or rewrite the rules as our own modules before launch (see [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md) section 4). Until then, keep its name and its claims off the site, which they currently are.
